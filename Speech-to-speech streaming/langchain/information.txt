Black Holes 
A black hole is a region in space where gravity is so strong that nothing, not even light, can escape it. They form when massive stars run out of fuel and collapse under their own gravity. At the center of a black hole is the singularity, a point of infinite density where space and time break down. Surrounding it is the event horizon, the boundary beyond which nothing can return. Black holes are invisible, but we detect them by observing the effects of their gravity on nearby stars and gas.  

Black holes come in different sizes:  
1.Stellar Black Holes: Formed by collapsing stars.  
2.Supermassive Black Holes: Found at the centers of galaxies, millions or billions of times heavier than the Sun.  

In 2019, the first image of a black hole was captured, revealing a glowing ring of gas around its event horizon.  

Wormholes
A wormhole is a theoretical shortcut through space-time, connecting two distant points in the universe. It’s like a tunnel that could allow instant travel across vast distances. Wormholes were first theorized by Einstein and Rosen, called Einstein-Rosen bridges.  

However, wormholes are purely hypothetical. To remain open and stable, they would require exotic matter with negative energy, something we’ve never observed. They might also collapse instantly if they exist at all.  

In theory, wormholes could allow not just space travel but even time travel, sparking debates about paradoxes and the nature of time. Though not proven, wormholes inspire both scientific inquiry and science fiction.
